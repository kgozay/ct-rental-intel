## Anti-Patterns Verdict

The app is visually distinctive enough to avoid generic SaaS styling. Its Electric & Ink identity is memorable. The weakness is restraint: the same thick borders, offset shadows, uppercase labels, badges, and repeated cards are applied almost everywhere. This makes the interface feel designed but not fully edited. The seven identical suburb cards are the clearest template-like moment.

The deterministic scan found three warnings: one genuine side-stripe callout in `src/components/AIPanel.jsx:193`, and two low-priority Helvetica warnings in `src/components/MapView.jsx:160` and `src/index.css:10`. The font warnings are effectively one intentional system-sans choice and are not a meaningful violation for this product. Browser overlay injection was unavailable because the browser evaluation environment was read-only, so no reliable visible overlay was created.

## Overall Impression

The landing page communicates the value proposition with energy, and the Rent Barometer is the strongest product moment. The dashboard then loses that decisiveness by presenting more than 20 controls, five tabs, and five KPIs before the first listing. The biggest opportunity is to shift from a data cockpit to a guided decision workspace: show what is fresh, why a listing is good value, and what the user should do next.

## What's Working

1. The Rent Barometer turns the promise into a personalized answer and carries the selected filters into the dashboard URL.
2. The visual voice is specific: cobalt, yellow, dotted paper, blunt type, and tenant-first language make the product memorable.
3. The functional foundation is thoughtful: shareable filters, CSV export, shortlist persistence, keyboard-sortable headers, reduced-motion support, skeletons, and view-specific empty states are already present.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Live, cached, baseline, and unavailable data are indistinguishable

**Why it matters:** Trust is the product. When the API fails, the landing page silently substitutes hard-coded medians while the dashboard says `Scraped: Never`. Users cannot tell whether a number is current evidence, an estimate, or unavailable.

**Fix:** Introduce one global data-status model showing source, last successful scrape, listing count, sample window, and explicit `Live`, `Cached`, `Baseline estimate`, or `Unavailable` states. Replace the zero dashboard with a guided first-run panel that explains what Refresh does, how long it takes, and its cooldown. Surface fetch failures instead of silently rendering zeros.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden dashboard data states`

### [P1] Core selection and listing-detail interactions are not accessibility-complete

**Why it matters:** Filter choices are primarily visual. The tab list lacks arrow-key behavior and panel relationships. Clickable table rows cannot be opened by keyboard. The drawer has dialog semantics but no initial focus, focus trap, or focus restoration. At the measured desktop viewport, many controls are only 24 to 32 pixels tall.

**Fix:** Use radio groups or `aria-pressed` for selection filters, implement complete tabs semantics, expose a focusable listing-detail action, and use a focus-managed drawer. Replace title-only help with a focusable popover or inline explanation. Standardize visible focus and enlarge frequent touch targets.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit dashboard accessibility`

### [P1] Dashboard density hides the primary job

**Why it matters:** Search, eight suburb choices, five price presets plus a slider, five tabs, and five KPIs arrive before any listing. Nearly every element carries a black outline or shadow, so the eye cannot distinguish primary from secondary actions.

**Fix:** Lead with a compact search intent block: location, budget, beds, and one clear results action. Collapse secondary filters, move view switching to the results header, reduce the first-row KPIs to two or three decision-relevant measures, and flatten low-priority controls.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill dashboard`

### [P2] Shortlisting stops before the actual rental decision

**Why it matters:** Users can save and filter favorites, but cannot compare trade-offs, record notes, or share a considered shortlist.

**Fix:** Add a shortlist workspace with side-by-side comparison, notes, difference highlighting, and share or export. Keep saved searches separate from saved listings, then surface new matches and price drops against those searches.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape shortlist comparison workspace`

### [P2] The visual system is applied too uniformly

**Why it matters:** Signature styling loses impact when every section, filter, card, and callout uses the same treatment. The landing page also lacks a strong Cape Town or property-specific visual anchor.

**Fix:** Preserve the expressive treatment for the hero, primary actions, barometer, and value verdicts. Flatten supporting content, replace the seven-card coverage grid with a spatial neighborhood view, add one authentic listing or city image, and vary section rhythm.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable quieter landing page and dashboard chrome`

## Persona Red Flags

### Jordan, first-time renter

Jordan understands the hero and barometer, then reaches a dashboard showing zeros, dashes, `Scraped: Never`, and Refresh. It is unclear whether Refresh reloads the page or launches a long, credit-limited data collection job. Five analytical tabs appear equally important, with no recommended starting path.

### Alex, experienced rental researcher

Alex benefits from URL-backed filters, sorting, CSV, and Property24 links, but cannot save searches, compare shortlisted listings, bulk-export selected rows, or move through detail views by keyboard. The fourth KPI also changes from Good Value to Shortlisted after the first save, removing a stable benchmark.

### Sam, keyboard and screen-reader user

Sam cannot open clickable listing rows by keyboard. Filter and barometer selection states are not consistently announced. The tiny hover-only value help is inaccessible on touch. The drawer does not manage focus. Several 10 to 11 pixel muted labels use low-opacity text that falls below normal-text contrast requirements.

## Minor Observations

- The dashboard logo and separate Home link lead to the same place.
- The map says all listings were filtered out even when no source data has ever loaded.
- `Available before` currently allows listings with an unknown availability date through.
- Best Value Suburb can be derived from only two listings without a confidence or sample-size warning.
- The landing platform-tour table uses sample data but does not label it as an example.
- Claims such as `100% Unbiased` and `every single unit` are stronger than the visible methodology supports.
- Emoji icons render inconsistently and weaken the analytical tone.
- The local Vite command starts only the frontend. API requests proxy to an absent server and fail, which makes local product evaluation look like a legitimate zero-data state.
- The production build succeeds and the domain test suite passes 16 of 16 tests, but lint currently fails with five errors.

## Questions to Consider

- Is the product fundamentally a continuously refreshed market service or a user-triggered scraping tool?
- What evidence would make a renter trust Good Value enough to contact an agent: recency, sample size, comparables, confidence, or all four?
- Should the default dashboard optimize for finding one promising listing quickly, or for open-ended market analysis?
